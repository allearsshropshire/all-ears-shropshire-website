Looking after your ears

After your appointment

Your hearing may improve straight away. A mild echo or slight itch can occur and often settles. Follow your personalised aftercare advice, including keeping your ears dry and avoiding swimming for 24 hours if advised.

Please get in touch if you have persistent pain, discharge, a sudden change in hearing or ongoing dizziness. Sudden hearing loss or severe symptoms need urgent medical assessment.

Your wellbeing matters

Your Safety Comes First

Before any wax removal, we ask about your ear health and examine your ears to help decide whether treatment is suitable. We explain the options and ask for your consent before proceeding.

  • Tell us before your appointment if you have had ear surgery, a perforated eardrum, grommets, recurring ear infections, ear pain or discharge, or have previously had difficulties with ear wax removal.
  • Treatment is tailored to you. Microsuction, irrigation or manual removal may be considered depending on your ear examination and medical history. Not every method is suitable for every person.
  • We may advise against treatment if it would be unsafe or if your symptoms suggest another cause. Where appropriate, we will recommend that you contact your GP or another healthcare service.
  • Seek urgent medical advice for sudden hearing loss, severe ear pain, new facial weakness or significant dizziness. Do not wait for a routine wax-removal appointment.

We'll explain any findings and aftercare advice clearly, and you can ask questions at any stage. Wax removal can sometimes cause temporary discomfort or dizziness and, rarely, irritation, bleeding, infection or injury. We will discuss relevant risks before treatment.

Before your visit

Preparing for your appointment

  1. Avoid cotton buds. They can push wax deeper.
  2. Let us know about previous ear problems, ear surgery or ear infections.
  3. If you wear hearing aids, please have them nearby.
  4. If you're unsure about using ear drops beforehand, just ask when you book.
