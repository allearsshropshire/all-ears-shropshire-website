What Is Microsuction?

A clear guide to microsuction earwax removal during a home visit in Shrewsbury and surrounding Shropshire areas.

How does microsuction work?

Microsuction is a method of removing earwax using a small suction device while the ear canal is viewed with suitable equipment. The practitioner works under direct visual guidance and removes wax only where it is safe and appropriate to do so.

What happens at your appointment?

We begin by asking about your ear health and medical history, then examining your ears. If microsuction is suitable, the procedure and its risks are explained and your consent is requested. You may hear a suction noise and feel some movement or discomfort. You can ask to stop at any time.

Is microsuction suitable for everyone?

Not always. An ear examination and screening are important. Pain, discharge, suspected infection, certain ear conditions, or difficulty safely accessing wax may mean treatment is deferred or a referral is recommended.

Are there any risks?

Possible effects include temporary discomfort, dizziness, irritation or minor bleeding. The suction can be loud, and temporary or occasionally lasting changes in hearing or tinnitus are possible. Serious complications are uncommon but cannot be ruled out. Your practitioner should explain risks relevant to your ears before proceeding.

What if the wax cannot be removed?

Wax may be too hard, too deep or unsafe to remove in one visit. You may be advised about appropriate wax-softening preparations, a further appointment, or seeking medical assessment.

Important: This page provides general information only and is not a diagnosis or a substitute for an individual ear-health assessment. If you have sudden hearing loss, severe pain, bleeding or discharge, seek prompt medical advice.
